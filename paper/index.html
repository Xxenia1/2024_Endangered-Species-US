Abstract

Abstract

This project develops an interactive visual analytics dashboard for exploring endangered species and related environmental conditions across U.S. states. The system brings together state-level species records, environmental risk, land-cover composition, protected areas, population, and economic indicators in a coordinated interface.

Linked map, ranking, and state-report views support a progression from national overview to state-level detail. The project is designed for exploratory analysis: it helps users compare conditions, identify patterns worth investigating, and understand how derived indicators organize a complex collection of variables.

Context

Background and Motivation

Endangered species distribution cannot be understood through species counts alone. A state with many recorded species may also experience substantial human pressure, environmental risk, or uneven conservation protection. These conditions provide context for interpreting where biodiversity is visible and where additional attention may be warranted.

In practice, the relevant information is distributed across datasets with different structures, units, and time references. A single-variable map can reveal one pattern while making it difficult to compare related factors. This project therefore treats coordination between views as part of the analytical method.

Exploratory orientation

The dashboard is intended to support questions and comparisons, not to present a final causal explanation or replace ecological assessment.

Inquiry

Project Questions

The interface is organized around open-ended questions that guide exploration rather than formal hypotheses.

Where are endangered species concentrated?

Compare species counts and density while keeping geographic context visible.

How do environmental and socioeconomic conditions vary among states?

Switch indicator views to examine differences in habitat, population, and economic context.

Where do high biodiversity value and high human pressure overlap?

Use coordinated highlighting to look for states that merit closer inspection.

Which states exhibit combined ecological vulnerability and limited conservation protection?

Combine risk and conservation indicators as a starting point for prioritization discussions.

Interaction model

Visual and Interaction Design

The dashboard follows an overview-first strategy: the national map establishes spatial context, while details become available through state selection, ranking, and the report panel. This supports progressive disclosure without removing the surrounding comparison context.

Coordinated multiple views use a shared state identity. A state selected on the map or ranking chart is reflected in the report, while map and chart encodings complement one another rather than duplicating the same visual task.

  • Overview first, details on demand
  • Coordinated multiple views
  • Consistent state selection
  • Progressive disclosure
  • Map and chart complementarity
  • Direct comparison through ranking
  • State-level contextual reporting

Reflection

Discussion

The system places otherwise dispersed variables into one coordinated analysis environment. A national overview can lead into state-level detail without requiring users to leave the spatial context, and linked views reduce the cost of switching between a map, a ranking, and a report.

Derived indicators also provide a practical way to organize complex variables for exploration. They can help users formulate comparisons and identify states for closer review, while keeping the underlying variables visible in the report and documentation.

Interpretation boundary

This dashboard is designed for exploratory analysis. It cannot directly replace ecological protection decisions, and its composite indicators depend on variable selection, normalization, and modeling assumptions.

Scope

Limitations

  • State-level aggregation may hide local variation.
  • Different datasets may represent different years.
  • Composite indicators depend on modeling assumptions.
  • The dashboard does not establish causality.
  • Ecological conditions change over time.
  • No formal user evaluation has been conducted.

Closing

Conclusion

This project combines data processing, spatial analysis, visual encoding, interaction design, and Web development into an exploratory system for endangered species and environmental conditions across the United States. Its central contribution is a coordinated environment in which users can move between overview, comparison, and context while keeping interpretation boundaries visible.
